How to choose an e-bike

A quick read based on Ava's buying guide.

The three classes

Every US e-bike is one of three classes. You'll see it on each card.

Class 1Pedal assist, up to 20 mph

The motor helps only while you pedal. The easiest place to start.

Class 2Pedal assist plus throttle, up to 20 mph

A throttle gets you moving without pedaling. Handy from a stop or with a load.

Class 3Pedal assist, up to 28 mph

The fastest legal class. No throttle in California, and not allowed on bike-only off-road trails.

The three types on this list

Standard · $400 offCity and commuter

Light, upright and easy to handle. Right for most riders who don't haul a lot.

Cargo · $900 offBuilt to carry

Room for kids, groceries or tools, with stronger motors. Bigger and heavier.

Adaptive · $900 offFor mobility needs

Trikes, recumbents, tandems and options for wheelchair users. Wider than most bikes.

What's on the spec sheet

Motor
Hub motors sit in the wheel and feel like a tailwind. Mid-drive motors sit at the pedals and feel more natural on hills.
Torque (Nm)
Pulling power. More torque helps with hills and heavy loads.
Battery (Wh)
More watt-hours means more miles, but a heavier bike. A full charge takes 1 to 5 hours.
Charging safely
Use the charger that came with the bike, plugged straight into the wall. Don't leave it charging for days.
Max load
The most weight the bike can carry, including you.

Ask yourself first

  • Am I replacing a daily car trip?
  • Will I take it on a bus or train?
  • How far will I ride each day?
  • What will I carry: a laptop, kids, groceries, tools?
  • Who will handle maintenance?
  • Would a step-through frame or a third wheel help?

Then compare on

  • Power: enough for your hills and loads.
  • Fit: frame size, seat and handlebars. Test ride if you can.
  • Weight: one you can lift and move yourself.
  • Range: your normal day, with room to spare.
  • Accessories: racks, fenders, lights, phone mount.
  • Service: a local shop you trust for repairs.

You qualify by income if you're enrolled in CARE or FERA (PG&E) or EAP (Alameda Municipal Power). You also get up to $250 for a helmet, lock and lights.

Who can apply

  • You're 18 or older.
  • You live in Alameda County, or you're an Ava home customer in San Joaquin County (Tracy, Stockton, Lathrop).
  • One rebate per person, two per household.

How to get it

  1. Apply on Ava's Bike Electric page.
  2. Ava holds a random drawing each month. You're entered in three.
  3. If you're picked, Ava emails you a rebate code.
  4. Use it in person at a participating shop within 60 days.

Good to know

  • The price on each card is the bike's price minus the regular rebate.
  • Sales tax and shipping are extra. The rebate can't pay for them.
  • It only works in person on a new bike from Ava's list. Not online, and not on used bikes.
  • Call the shop first to check that they have the bike.
